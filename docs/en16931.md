# EN 16931 totals in plain JSON Schema

EN 16931, the European standard for electronic invoices, states its document totals as sums and differences of two-decimal amounts, and each VAT category's tax as its taxable amount times its rate. Schema Calc compiles eight of those rules, BR-CO-10 to BR-CO-17, into standard JSON Schema, which a stock validator runs on a flat JSON form of an invoice. The product in BR-CO-17 compiles because the rate is one of a listed few. The official Schematron checks the rules a little differently in each of the two syntaxes, UBL and CII, so there is one schema for each. This page compares their verdicts with the official Schematron's: on every example invoice published with the official validation artefacts, and on copies of them with amounts changed or optional elements removed.

**On all 804 invoices the verdicts are the same, rule by rule.** Each schema is 10.5 MB, most of it for adding up 20 signed line amounts. Python `jsonschema` runs them with its default options; Ajv runs them once their resources are added one at a time, as the playground does.

## The rules

| Rule | What must hold |
|---|---|
| BR-CO-10 | Sum of invoice line net amounts (BT-106) = Σ invoice line net amount (BT-131) |
| BR-CO-11 | Sum of allowances on document level (BT-107) = Σ document level allowance amount (BT-92) |
| BR-CO-12 | Sum of charges on document level (BT-108) = Σ document level charge amount (BT-99) |
| BR-CO-13 | Invoice total without VAT (BT-109) = Σ BT-131 − document allowances (BT-107) + document charges (BT-108) |
| BR-CO-14 | Invoice total VAT amount (BT-110) = Σ VAT category tax amount (BT-117) |
| BR-CO-15 | Invoice total with VAT (BT-112) = BT-109 + invoice total VAT amount (BT-110) |
| BR-CO-16 | Amount due for payment (BT-115) = BT-112 − paid amount (BT-113) + rounding amount (BT-114) |
| BR-CO-17 | VAT category tax amount (BT-117) = VAT category taxable amount (BT-116) × VAT category rate (BT-119) / 100, rounded to two decimals |

The rules do not say what happens when an amount is missing, and the two Schematrons decide that rule by rule. [ubl.json](../en16931/ubl.json) and [cii.json](../en16931/cii.json) state each rule as its Schematron checks it. A relation counts a missing amount as zero; `has x` and `no x` test whether the optional amount `x` is given, or whether the list `x` has an item:

| Rule | UBL | CII |
|---|---|---|
| BR-CO-10 | `lineNetTotal = sum(lines.netAmount)` | the same |
| BR-CO-11 | `has allowanceTotal and allowanceTotal = sum(allowances.amount) or no allowanceTotal and no allowances` | the same |
| BR-CO-12 | `has chargeTotal and chargeTotal = sum(charges.amount) or no chargeTotal and no charges` | the same |
| BR-CO-13 | `totalWithoutVat = lineNetTotal - allowanceTotal + chargeTotal` | the same |
| BR-CO-14 | `no vatBreakdown or vatTotal = sum(vatBreakdown.taxAmount)` | `no vatTotal or vatTotal = sum(vatBreakdown.taxAmount)` |
| BR-CO-15 | `has vatTotal and totalWithVat = totalWithoutVat + vatTotal` | `has vatTotal and totalWithVat = totalWithoutVat + vatTotal or totalWithVat = totalWithoutVat` |
| BR-CO-16 | `amountDue = totalWithVat - paidAmount + roundingAmount` | the same |
| BR-CO-17 | [below](#br-co-17-a-product-by-a-listed-rate), with 99.5 | the same, with 100.5 |

These come from the Schematrons' tests rather than from a summary of the rules:

- **A missing allowance or charge total is allowed only without allowances or charges.** BR-CO-11 and BR-CO-12 accept a missing BT-107 or BT-108 when the invoice has no document-level allowances or charges, and otherwise require the total, even when the amounts add up to zero.
- **BR-CO-13 and BR-CO-16 read a missing amount as zero.** Each Schematron spells out four cases, one for each combination of present and missing optional amounts; together they say what the relation says when a missing amount is zero.
- **BR-CO-14 checks only a VAT total it can find.** The UBL Schematron checks each VAT total that has a breakdown. The CII Schematron checks the VAT total in the invoice currency, where there is one, against the sum of every VAT category.
- **BR-CO-15 differs between the syntaxes.** The UBL Schematron requires exactly one VAT total in the document currency. The CII Schematron also accepts BT-112 = BT-109 whatever BT-110 is, which the rule's text does not.
- **BR-CO-13 uses BT-106**, the invoice's own sum of line net amounts, rather than adding the lines again. Where BR-CO-10 holds the two are the same; where it fails, only BR-CO-10 reports it, in both the Schematron and the schema.
- **BR-CO-16 includes the rounding amount**, BT-114, which can be negative.
- **BR-CO-10 to BR-CO-16 round to the cent.** All their amounts have at most two decimals, so the rounding never changes a result, and the schema checks exact equality.

Written as plain relations, without the Schematrons' conditions, the first seven rules gave the official verdict on all but six of the 475 invoices then compared, all six CII. `and`, `or`, `has` and `no` were added to the compiler to state the conditions.

### BR-CO-17: a product by a listed rate

Both Schematrons check each VAT category the same way, apart from one comparison. If the category has no VAT rate, or its rate rounds to zero, its tax amount must round to zero. Otherwise the tax amount, without its sign, must come within one currency unit of the taxable amount, without its sign, times the rate, rounded to the cent: strictly within in UBL, whose test uses `<` and `>`, and up to a whole unit in CII, whose test uses `<=` and `>=`. So "rounded to two decimals" leaves a tolerance of one unit, not one cent, and the two syntaxes part exactly at one unit.

Rounding is not linear, but here it only moves the bounds by half a cent. Write X for the rate times the taxable amount, without its sign, which is the tax in cents before rounding, and Y for 100 times the tax amount, without its sign. XPath's `round` takes halves up, so in UBL round(X) is within 99 cents of Y exactly when −99.5 ≤ X − Y < 99.5, and in CII within 100 cents exactly when −100.5 ≤ X − Y < 100.5. The UBL contract states it as

```
vatBreakdown.rate < 0.5 and vatBreakdown.taxAmount >= -0.5 and vatBreakdown.taxAmount < 0.5
or vatBreakdown.rate >= 0.5
  and vatBreakdown.rate * abs(vatBreakdown.taxableAmount) - 100 * abs(vatBreakdown.taxAmount) >= -99.5
  and vatBreakdown.rate * abs(vatBreakdown.taxableAmount) - 100 * abs(vatBreakdown.taxAmount) < 99.5
```

on one line, and the CII contract with 100.5. It holds on every VAT category, as a constraint written with `vatBreakdown.field` does. Three parts of the language make it possible:

- **A field with listed values can multiply another field.** The contract lists the rates that `vatBreakdown.rate` may take: 0, 6, 10, 12, 15, 19, 21, 22, 25 and 27, every rate in the official examples. For each rate, the compiler builds the relation with that rate as a whole-number coefficient, and an `anyOf` of the rate's values chooses among them. Each nonzero rate adds about 95 kB, and BR-CO-17 alone takes 874 kB for the ten.
- **`abs(x)`** reads an amount without its sign, as the Schematron's `abs` does. It costs nothing: a signed amount's machine tests the minus sign at every digit it reads, and `abs` leaves those tests out.
- **An optional field of a line counts as zero.** A category whose rate is missing, or whose tax scheme or type code is not VAT, has no rate in the Schematron's reading, and no `rate` in the JSON form. Zero and a missing rate ask the same of the tax amount, so the first clause covers both.

The form accepts only the listed rates, so a VAT category at any other rate fails the schema, whatever the Schematron says. A contract for real invoices lists the rates its sellers charge.

## The JSON form

Both contracts declare the same form:

| JSON | Business term | UBL | CII | |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `lineNetTotal` | BT-106 | `LegalMonetaryTotal/LineExtensionAmount` | `LineTotalAmount` | required |
| `allowanceTotal` | BT-107 | `LegalMonetaryTotal/AllowanceTotalAmount` | `AllowanceTotalAmount` | optional |
| `chargeTotal` | BT-108 | `LegalMonetaryTotal/ChargeTotalAmount` | `ChargeTotalAmount` | optional |
| `totalWithoutVat` | BT-109 | `LegalMonetaryTotal/TaxExclusiveAmount` | `TaxBasisTotalAmount` | required |
| `vatTotal` | BT-110 | `TaxTotal/TaxAmount` in the document currency | `TaxTotalAmount` in the invoice currency | optional |
| `totalWithVat` | BT-112 | `LegalMonetaryTotal/TaxInclusiveAmount` | `GrandTotalAmount` | required |
| `paidAmount` | BT-113 | `LegalMonetaryTotal/PrepaidAmount` | `TotalPrepaidAmount` | optional |
| `roundingAmount` | BT-114 | `LegalMonetaryTotal/PayableRoundingAmount` | `RoundingAmount` | optional |
| `amountDue` | BT-115 | `LegalMonetaryTotal/PayableAmount` | `DuePayableAmount` | required |
| `lines[].netAmount` | BT-131 | `InvoiceLine/LineExtensionAmount` | `SpecifiedTradeSettlementLineMonetarySummation/LineTotalAmount` | each line |
| `allowances[].amount` | BT-92 | `AllowanceCharge/Amount` with `ChargeIndicator` false | `SpecifiedTradeAllowanceCharge/ActualAmount` with `ChargeIndicator` false | each allowance |
| `charges[].amount` | BT-99 | `AllowanceCharge/Amount` with `ChargeIndicator` true | `SpecifiedTradeAllowanceCharge/ActualAmount` with `ChargeIndicator` true | each charge |
| `vatBreakdown[].taxAmount` | BT-117 | `TaxSubtotal/TaxAmount` of that VAT total | `ApplicableTradeTax/CalculatedAmount` | each VAT category |
| `vatBreakdown[].taxableAmount` | BT-116 | `TaxSubtotal/TaxableAmount` | `ApplicableTradeTax/BasisAmount` | each VAT category |
| `vatBreakdown[].rate` | BT-119 | `TaxSubtotal/TaxCategory/Percent`, when its `TaxScheme/ID` is VAT | `ApplicableTradeTax/RateApplicablePercent`, when its `TypeCode` is VAT | optional; a listed rate |

The CII elements are in the header or line trade settlement summation. Each amount is a decimal string with exactly two fractional digits and an optional minus sign, such as `"-25.00"`, with at most six integer digits. A rate is written the same way, so 25 % is `"25.00"`. An optional amount may be absent. The four lists are always present, and may be empty: an invoice has at most 20 lines and at most five allowances, charges and VAT categories each. An item may carry other properties, such as a line's `id`, an allowance's reason code or a VAT category's code; only its amounts are checked. The second UBL example becomes:

```json
{
  "lineNetTotal": "1436.50", "allowanceTotal": "100.00", "chargeTotal": "100.00",
  "totalWithoutVat": "1436.50", "vatTotal": "365.28", "totalWithVat": "1801.78",
  "paidAmount": "1000.00", "amountDue": "801.78",
  "lines": [
    { "id": "1", "netAmount": "1273.00" }, { "id": "2", "netAmount": "-3.96" },
    { "id": "3", "netAmount": "4.96" }, { "id": "4", "netAmount": "-25.00" },
    { "id": "5", "netAmount": "187.50" }
  ],
  "allowances": [{ "reason": "88", "amount": "100.00" }],
  "charges": [{ "amount": "100.00" }],
  "vatBreakdown": [
    { "category": "S", "taxAmount": "365.13", "taxableAmount": "1460.50", "rate": "25.00" },
    { "category": "S", "taxAmount": "0.15", "taxableAmount": "1.00", "rate": "15.00" },
    { "category": "E", "taxAmount": "0.00", "taxableAmount": "-25.00", "rate": "0.00" }
  ]
}
```

[convert.py](../en16931/convert.py) writes this form from UBL or CII XML using only Python's standard library. It copies each business term and rewrites its decimal with two fractional digits, so `130` becomes `"130.00"`; it never computes a total or a tax. An allowance or charge is read as the Schematron reads its `ChargeIndicator`, so `0` and `1` count as false and true. A value that two digits cannot hold exactly, or one with more than six integer digits, is copied unchanged, so the schema rejects its format.

## Agreement

The official artefacts are release [validation-1.3.17](https://github.com/ConnectingEurope/eInvoicing-EN16931/releases/tag/validation-1.3.17), whose Schematron is published compiled to XSLT. Saxon-HE runs it on each XML invoice. The JSON form of the same invoice is checked by its syntax's schema for all eight rules, and by a schema for each rule alone, all in Ajv.

Besides each unchanged example, the comparison adds one cent to each total the invoice has, one at a time, and to the first item of each list that has one. Six further changes keep one rule true while breaking another: the first line together with BT-106, which breaks only BR-CO-13; the first allowance with BT-107, and the first charge with BT-108, which also break only BR-CO-13; the first VAT category with BT-110, which breaks only BR-CO-15; BT-112 together with BT-115, which breaks only BR-CO-15; and BT-109, BT-112 and BT-115 together, which breaks only BR-CO-13. Other copies remove optional elements: each optional total the invoice has (BT-107, BT-108, BT-110, BT-113, BT-114); every allowance, every charge, or the whole VAT breakdown; and each of those lists together with its total. Removing a UBL VAT total removes its `TaxTotal`, so every copy stays valid against its syntax's XML schema. Five more copies test BR-CO-17 alone. Four move the first VAT category's tax amount by 1.00, −1.00, 0.50 or −0.50, and BT-110, BT-112 and BT-115 by the same, so that BR-CO-14 to BR-CO-16 still hold: a unit is where the two tolerances part, and half a unit is where a tax without a rate stops rounding to zero. The fifth removes the first category's rate. The verdicts are the same when the rules among these eight that the Schematron reports as failed are exactly those whose schemas reject the JSON, and the schema for all eight rejects exactly when the Schematron reports any of them.

**Every verdict agrees**, for each rule separately and for all eight together, in both syntaxes. Python `jsonschema` with its default options gives the same verdicts as Ajv on every invoice.

The removals are where the bindings part. Without its VAT breakdown, every UBL invoice still passes BR-CO-14, since its VAT total has no breakdown left to check, while 11 of the 15 CII invoices fail it; the other four, `CII-BR-CO-10-RoundingIssue.xml`, `CII_business_example_Z.xml`, `CII_example7.xml` and `XRechnung-O.xml`, have a VAT total of zero or none. Without its VAT total, every UBL invoice fails BR-CO-15, and so do the CII invoices except the two whose VAT total was zero, which pass by BT-112 = BT-109.

Every unchanged example passes BR-CO-17, and each of the 75 invoices that fail it fails no other of the eight rules. A tax amount a whole unit off is where the syntaxes part. UBL rejects all 38 such copies. CII rejects 5 of 26: the two zero-rated invoices, `CII-BR-CO-10-RoundingIssue.xml` and `CII_business_example_Z.xml`, whose tax must round to zero, either way, and `huf_example_cii.xml` raised by 1.00, whose tax of 18,679.00 was already 0.40 above 27 % of 69,180.00. Half a unit fails only a category without a rate or at zero, and only upwards, since XPath rounds −0.50 to zero and 0.50 to one: two copies in each syntax. Removing the first category's rate fails every invoice whose first tax amount is not zero.

The times compare unlike work: the Schematron checks all of EN 16931's rules, and each schema eight. Preparing a schema is the expensive part. Adding its resources to Ajv bottom-up, as the playground does ([ajv-validator.mjs](../src/ajv-validator.mjs)), takes under 20 seconds; Ajv's own `compile` takes several minutes, against half a second for Saxon to compile the XSLT. Once prepared, Ajv checks an invoice in under a millisecond, about as fast as Saxon. Python `jsonschema` prepares lazily and pays per invoice instead, about a second each.

## Limits

- **At most 20 lines and six integer digits.** BR-CO-10 adds up every line in a fixed decision graph. In each column it reads how many lines have each digit, and how many of those are negative, with `contains`, `minContains` and `maxContains` ([design.md](design.md#domain)); the schema grows faster than the number of lines, and linearly with the number of digits. BR-CO-11, -12 and -14 add up at most five items each, at about 0.7 MB apiece. The three rules without a list take 549 kB in UBL and 573 kB in CII, whose BR-CO-15 has a second relation; BR-CO-17 takes 874 kB, and the seven rules without BR-CO-10 take 3.6 MB in UBL and 3.7 MB in CII.
- **Close to the validators' stack limits.** Python `jsonschema` needs about 475 stack frames for a 20-line invoice, within its default of 1,000; reading every line position in turn needed over 1,700. Ajv's own `compile` turns a document this size into functions too large for Node's default stack: with default options it compiles in several minutes and then overflows while validating, and with `inlineRefs: false` validations overflow too. Adding the resources to Ajv bottom-up, each compiled on its own ([ajv-validator.mjs](../src/ajv-validator.mjs)), avoids both.
- **What the form leaves out.** It has one VAT total, in the document currency, and assumes the invoice states that currency, as BR-05 requires. Without a currency code the converter finds no VAT total, while both Schematrons skip BR-CO-15. A VAT total in another currency, BT-111, is not in the form, though the UBL Schematron's BR-CO-14 and BR-CO-17 would also check a breakdown under it. Two VAT totals in the document currency, or any amount repeated where the XML schema allows one, become a list that the schema rejects. `run.mjs` reports every invoice whose JSON form has a missing required total, a list longer than its maximum or an amount it cannot hold; none of the 804 does.
- **Only the listed rates.** BR-CO-17 compiles one relation for each rate the contract lists, about 95 kB each. A rate outside the list fails the schema; a rate the Schematron would accept, such as 7 or 20, has to be added to the list.
- **Other products are out of reach.** A line's net amount is its quantity times its price, and neither is drawn from a short list. A product of two free fields is not a linear relation: the compiler reads digits column by column with a bounded carry, and multiplying two eight-digit numbers that way would need state for every partial product.

## Reproduce it

```sh
en16931/fetch.sh              # official artefacts at validation-1.3.17, and Saxon-HE 12.5
node en16931/run.mjs --python # needs Python 3 and a JDK; --write updates this page and results.json
```
